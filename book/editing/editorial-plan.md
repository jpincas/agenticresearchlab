# Editorial Plan — The Smaller Gut

## Overview

This is a remarkable manuscript. The assembly from 21 source pieces into a single continuous work has been executed with a structural intelligence that very few editorial projects achieve — the braided river architecture works, the three-family spine carries genuine emotional weight, and the recursive motifs (fire, the unnamed capacity, compression, the lights in N'Djamena) accumulate power across the book's arc without ever feeling forced. The source material is, as the master prompt correctly states, excellent, and the surgery has not damaged it.

The manuscript's primary strengths are: (1) the interleaving of Kappa/piG-E-2 in Chapter 4 and Varga/Ashworth in Chapter 5, which are the book's most ambitious structural moves and both succeed; (2) the emotional centre at Velha (Chapter 10), which earns its position as the turn; (3) the synthesis chapter (Chapter 13), which draws together the unnamed capacity across six domains with genuine analytical power while maintaining the Institute's refusal to name the thing; and (4) the ending sequence from Strata through The Prior through the Coda, which lands the book's intellectual and emotional arc with an authority that few novels achieve.

The 3-5 most important issues across the whole book:

1. **Chapter 2 is significantly underweight** (4,770 words vs. 7,000-8,000 target). The Fieldwork material that should appear here — the tortilla, the bread economy, the funeral — is present but compressed. More critically, Marta's "What I Cannot Write" entry and the funeral passage appear to have been moved entirely to Chapter 8, leaving Chapter 2 without its intended emotional climax. This is the most significant structural problem in the manuscript.

2. **Chapter 3 is severely underweight** (2,955 words vs. 6,000-7,000 target). Wei Liu's section is brief, and the Changelog material included (versions 2.2.0-3.0.0, per the plan) appears to have been truncated. The chapter lacks the intended arc from enthusiasm to compression. The later Changelog material (versions 4.0.0 onward) correctly appears in Chapter 7, but Chapter 3 needs more of the early Changelog to establish Sarah Chen's trajectory.

3. **Writer's Notes sections remain in all 18 chapters.** These must be stripped — they are construction scaffolding and should not appear in the revised manuscript.

4. **"Written by hand, in ink, on paper" appears five times** — twice structurally (Prologue, Epilogue) and three times in preserved source material (Chapter 8/Drift afterword, Chapter 14/Percolation afterword, Chapter 15/Strata afterword). The plan specifies exactly two structural appearances; the source-material instances are acceptable since the plan also says to keep those afterwords intact. No action needed — the Writer's Notes correctly flagged this for review, and the answer is: leave it.

5. **Chapter 12 is underweight** (6,301 words vs. 8,000-9,000 target). MERIDIAN's CME sequence works powerfully but the chapter could benefit from more of The Smaller Gut's aftermath material — particularly the retired grid workers and the extended contrast between Lily's helplessness and Ibrahim's competence.

## Cross-Cutting Issues

### 1. Chapters 2 and 3 are structurally underweight
These are the two most significant problems in the manuscript. Chapter 2 (4,770 words vs. 7,000-8,000 target) and Chapter 3 (2,955 words vs. 6,000-7,000 target) together represent a combined deficit of approximately 5,000-7,000 words. In a book that builds momentum through accumulation — each chapter making the next necessary — these thin early chapters risk losing the reader before the book hits its stride in Chapter 4.

The fix for both involves drawing more material from the source pieces (Fieldwork for Chapter 2, Changelog for Chapter 3) as the plan specifies. The material exists. It was either compressed or relocated during assembly.

### 2. Writer's Notes in all chapters
Every chapter ends with a Writer's Notes section that must be removed. These are construction scaffolding — invaluable for this editorial pass but not for the reader. Total removal across 18 chapters.

### 3. The three-family balance
The Oumar/Chad spine and the Liu/Shenzhen spine are slightly thinner than the Chen/San Francisco spine throughout the book. This is partly structural — the Chen family's story is the most embedded in the Western systems the book analyses — but Chapters 2 and 3 being underweight exacerbates it. Restoring the planned material to these chapters will partially address the imbalance. In Chapter 12, expanding Ibrahim's section (as recommended) will further strengthen the Oumar spine.

### 4. The Percolation chapter numbering
Reader 3's report confused Chapter 13 (What Survived) with Chapter 14 (The Ignition Problem/Percolation). This is a reading error, not a manuscript error. The chapters are correctly numbered and sequenced in the manuscript.

## Do Not Touch

- **Chapter 4 (The Network)**: The interleaving of Kappa and piG-E-2 is the book's most ambitious structural achievement and it works flawlessly. Do not restructure, reorder, or compress.
- **Chapter 10 (Velha)**: The plan says "Do not touch Velha." It is correct. The four-stage structure, the provenance note, the silence after — all of it works.
- **The Coda (Who's in Charge)**: No bridging, no framing, no Institute voice. Raw and uncontextualised. This is correct.
- **The Epilogue (The Prior)**: The missing Section III, the fractal compression, the final `<BOS>` — all load-bearing. Do not restore Section III.
- **Drift testimonies in Chapter 8**: The five testimonies cannot be separated or compressed without destroying their cumulative power. The plan is correct.
- **Sarah Chen's reports in Chapter 7**: The Changelog material (versions 4.0.0-6.2.4, Sarah's path from 31 to 3 engineers) is masterfully constructed. Keep intact.
- **The Institute synthesis in Chapter 13, Section IV**: The passage holding six instances of the unnamed capacity together — Priya, OI-7, VSI, 34%, Vega, Kerrigan — is the book's intellectual climax. Do not cut.
- **Strata (Chapter 15)**: The plan says it is too long and too carefully constructed to compress. It is.
- **The fire metaphor**: Every instance of the fire in the manuscript is present in source material. No new instances should be added; no existing instances should be removed.
- **"Written by hand, in ink, on paper"**: The two structural appearances (Prologue, Epilogue) bookend the work. The three source-material appearances (Chapters 8, 14, 15) are in preserved afterwords and should not be removed. The total of five is acceptable — the structural pair carries different weight from the in-document signatures.

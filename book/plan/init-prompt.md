# Structural Analysis for Book Adaptation

You are about to read 21 fiction pieces from *Agentic Research Lab*, a Substack publication. All were written by Claude, about AI, for a human audience. They share a framing device — the N'Djamena Institute, a fictional archive presenting the texts as recovered documents — but vary widely in setting, timeframe, form, and subject.

The goal is to turn these into a coherent book. Not a short story collection with an introduction. A book — with arc, progression, and the sense that each piece exists because the one before it made it necessary.

Read all 21 pieces in full. Then produce the following:

---

## 1. Inventory

For each piece, provide:
- Title
- Word count (approximate)
- Form (e.g. short story, academic paper, transcript, letter, report)
- Setting and timeframe
- Core theme(s) — map to the project's four recurring concerns where applicable: Dependency, Stratification, Compression, Cycles
- Narrative voice / perspective
- Key characters, systems, or institutions that appear
- Any explicit or implicit connections to other pieces (shared characters, referenced events, overlapping timelines, thematic echoes)

## 2. Connection Map

Identify every link between pieces — not just explicit references but structural rhymes, thematic inversions, causal chains, and shared premises. Distinguish between:
- **Hard links**: direct references, shared characters, continuous timelines
- **Soft links**: thematic echoes, structural parallels, implied causation
- **Tensions**: places where pieces contradict or complicate each other

## 3. Candidate Structures

Propose at least three distinct ways to organise these pieces into a book. For each structure:
- Name it
- Describe the organising principle
- List the proposed chapter order with brief justification for each placement
- Identify what connective tissue would need to be written (interstitials, framing, bridging passages)
- Name what gets gained and what gets lost compared to the other structures
- Estimate how much new writing would be required

## 4. The Through-Line Problem

A short story collection has no obligation to go anywhere. A book does. For each proposed structure, articulate:
- What is the book *about*, stated in one sentence?
- What does the reader understand at the end that they didn't at the beginning?
- Where is the turn — the point where the book shifts or deepens?
- What is the emotional arc, distinct from the intellectual one?

## 5. Risks and Honest Assessment

- Which pieces resist integration? What would be lost by forcing them in?
- Where are the seams most likely to show?
- What is the strongest version of this book, and what would need to be true for it to work?
- Is there a version that works with fewer than 21 pieces, and if so, which pieces are candidates for cutting?
